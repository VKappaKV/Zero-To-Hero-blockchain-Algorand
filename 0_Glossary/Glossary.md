### GLOSSARY

## A

- **Address**: A unique identifier used to send and receive cryptocurrency. Typically represented as a string of alphanumeric characters (e.g., a Bitcoin address).
- **Airdrop**: A distribution of a cryptocurrency token or coin, usually for free, to numerous wallet addresses as a promotional strategy or to distribute a new token.
- **AMM**: Automated Market Maker, algorithm used in Decentralized Exchanges to perform trading through different mathematical formulas instead of bids and orders like in Centralized Order Books.
- **ASIC (Application-Specific Integrated Circuit)**: A type of hardware specifically designed to perform a single task. In crypto, ASICs are specialized for mining.
- **AVM**: Algorand Virtual Machine, execution environment for transactions and logic processed on the Algorand Blockchain


- **Account Abstraction**: A model where user accounts are smart-contract based, enabling custom validation logic and gas payment mechanisms.
- **Atomic Swap**: A trustless exchange of assets across chains or assets using hashed timelock contracts (HTLCs), without intermediaries.
- **Attestation**: A signed statement or vote used as evidence (e.g., validator attestations for consensus or identity claims).
## B

- **Block**: A unit of data that contains transactions. Blocks are linked together to form a blockchain.
- **Block Production**: Time required by the block proposer to create the block and gather enough signature to add it to the chain. Don't confuse it with block finality!
- **Blockchain**: A distributed ledger technology that records transactions in a secure, transparent, and immutable way, using cryptographic techniques.
- **Block Explorer**: A tool or website used to view and analyze details of blocks, addresses, and transactions on a specific blockchain.
- **Block Reward**: The incentive that miners receive for successfully solving a cryptographic puzzle and validating a block of transactions.
- **Bridge**: A technology that connects two separate blockchains, allowing for the transfer of tokens or data between them.
- **Burn**: permanently removing tokens from circulation


- **Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT)**: The ability of a distributed system to reach agreement even when some participants act arbitrarily or maliciously (e.g., PBFT, Tendermint).
- **Beacon Chain**: The Proof-of-Stake consensus layer of Ethereum introduced during the Merge, coordinating validators and finality.
- **Bonding Curve**: A formula-driven pricing mechanism that mints/burns tokens along a curve based on supply.
## C

- **Consensus Mechanism**: The algorithm used by a blockchain network to agree on the validity of transactions. Common types include Proof of Work (PoW) and Proof of Stake (PoS).
- **Cryptography**: The practice of secure communication through encoding information. In blockchain, cryptographic techniques secure data and control the creation of new units of cryptocurrency.
- **Custodial Wallet**: A type of wallet where a third party holds and manages the private keys on behalf of the user.


- **Cold Wallet (Cold Storage)**: A wallet kept offline to reduce the risk of compromise; often hardware or paper wallets.
- **Composability**: The property that protocols and smart contracts can combine like 'money legos' to create new functionality.
- **Cross-Chain Messaging**: Mechanisms that pass data/commands between blockchains, beyond simple token bridging.
- **Coinbase Transaction**: The first transaction in a block that pays the block reward and collects fees to the block producer.
- **Commitment Scheme**: A cryptographic primitive that lets one commit to a value while keeping it hidden, with the ability to reveal later.
- **Cryptoeconomics**: The study of incentive design and economic mechanisms in cryptographic, decentralized systems.
## D

- **DAO (Decentralized Autonomous Organization)**: An organization represented by rules encoded as a computer program that is transparent and controlled by shareholders or token holders, rather than a central authority.
- **dApp (Decentralized Application)**: An application that operates on a blockchain or peer-to-peer network, without a central authority.
- **Decentralization**: The distribution of authority, information, or infrastructure across a network, rather than being concentrated in one central entity.
- **DeFi (Decentralized Finance)**: A movement that leverages blockchain technology to recreate and improve traditional financial systems (e.g., lending, borrowing) without intermediaries like banks.
- **DEX**: Decentralized Exchange, a kind of DeFi protocol for trading.
- **Double Spend**: A flaw in digital currencies where the same cryptocurrency is spent more than once. Blockchain technology aims to prevent this through consensus mechanisms.


- **Distributed Key Generation (DKG)**: A protocol by which multiple parties jointly create a key pair without any single party learning the private key.
- **Deterministic Execution**: Given the same inputs and state, a smart contract produces the same outputs, enabling consensus across nodes.
- **Dust**: A very small token balance that is uneconomical to transfer due to fees.
## E

- **Encryption**: The process of encoding information so that only authorized parties can read it.
- **ERC-20**: A technical standard for smart contracts on the Ethereum blockchain. ERC-20 tokens are fungible and follow specific rules.
- **ERC-721**: A technical standard for non-fungible tokens (NFTs) on the Ethereum blockchain, used to create unique or distinct digital assets.
- **EVM**: Ethereum Virtual Machine, execution environment for transactions and logic processed on the Ethereum Blockchain


- **Ethereum Name Service (ENS)**: A naming system mapping human‑readable names (e.g., alice.eth) to addresses and other resources.
- **ECDSA**: Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm widely used for transaction signatures in many blockchains.
- **EdDSA**: A modern digital signature scheme (e.g., Ed25519) used for efficiency and security in some blockchains.
- **Epoch**: A fixed time window used for validator rotations, reward accounting, and checkpoints.
- **Exit (L2 Exit)**: The process of withdrawing funds or state from a Layer 2 back to the Layer 1 chain.
## F

- **FIAT**: traditional government-issued currency
- **Finality**: term used to identify a state for a block or transaction when it can't be rolled back and it's permanently part of the blockchain history
- **Fork**: A change in the blockchain protocol. A "hard fork" creates a new chain that is not compatible with the old one, while a "soft fork" is backward-compatible.
- **Full Node**: A participant in the blockchain network that keeps a full copy of the blockchain's history and validates transactions.


- **Flash Loan**: An uncollateralized loan that must be borrowed and repaid within a single transaction.
- **Front-Running**: Inserting a transaction ahead of a known pending transaction to extract profit (closely related to MEV).
- **Finality Gadget**: A component layered over a fork-choice rule that provides economic finality (e.g., Ethereum's Casper FFG).
- **Fee Market**: The mechanism by which transaction fees are determined and prioritized (e.g., EIP-1559 base fee + tips).
## G

- **Gas**: A unit that measures the computational effort required to execute operations on the Ethereum network. Users pay gas fees to miners for processing their transactions.
- **Genesis Block**: The first block in a blockchain, from which all subsequent blocks follow.


- **Gas Limit**: The maximum amount of gas allowed per transaction or per block.
- **Gas Price**: The fee offered per unit of gas to incentivize inclusion of a transaction.
- **Gwei**: A denomination of Ether equal to 10^-9 ETH, commonly used for gas prices.
- **Governance Token**: A token that confers on-chain voting rights over protocol parameters and treasury decisions.
## H

- **Hash**: A function that takes an input and generates a fixed-length output (a hash). In blockchain, hashes are used to secure transaction data.
- **Hash Rate**: The number of hashes (cryptographic computations) a miner can process per second. It is often used to measure the performance and security of a Proof-of-Work blockchain.


- **Hard Cap**: The maximum total supply of a token that will ever exist.
- **Hot Wallet**: A wallet connected to the internet, convenient for frequent use but with higher risk exposure.
- **HTLC (Hashed Timelock Contract)**: A contract that requires providing a preimage of a hash before a deadline, enabling atomic payments/swaps.
## I

- **ICO (Initial Coin Offering)**: A fundraising mechanism where new cryptocurrency projects sell tokens to investors, similar to an IPO in the stock market.
- **Interoperability**: The ability of different blockchain networks to communicate and share information with each other.


- **Immutable**: Data or code that cannot be changed once written on-chain; often contrasted with upgradable contracts.
- **Inflationary/Deflationary**: Token supply models where supply increases (inflationary) or decreases (deflationary) over time.
- **Indexer**: A service that parses blockchain data into queryable formats/APIs for dApps (e.g., The Graph).
- **Intents**: User‑declared desired outcomes that solvers/relayers fulfill, often enabling better UX and MEV-resilient execution.
## L

- **Layer 1**: The base protocol of a blockchain (like Bitcoin or Ethereum), responsible for security, consensus, and transaction processing.
- **Layer 2**: A secondary framework built on top of a Layer 1 blockchain to increase efficiency and scalability, often through off-chain solutions like payment channels.
- **Latency**: time delay between two actions. It's usually used for block latency to understand the amount of time between each block production. Not to be confused with block finality.
- **Ledger**: A record-keeping system for transactions. In blockchain, the ledger is distributed and immutable.
- **Liquidity**: The ease with which an asset can be converted into cash or another asset without affecting its price. In DeFi, liquidity pools are often used to ensure sufficient liquidity.


- **L2 Sequencer**: The entity (or set of entities) that orders transactions and proposes blocks for a Layer 2 rollup.
- **Liveness**: A property of consensus systems guaranteeing the network continues to make progress under assumptions.
- **Light Client**: A node that verifies block headers and proofs without storing full state, enabling resource‑constrained verification.
- **Liquidity Mining**: Distributing tokens as incentives to users who provide liquidity to a protocol.
- **Liquid Staking Token (LST)**: A token representing a claim on staked assets, retaining liquidity while earning staking yield.
## M

- **Mempool**: waiting data structure where transactions are stored before being included in a block for consensus.
- **Merkle Tree**: A data structure used in blockchain to efficiently and securely verify the integrity of large sets of data by storing them in a hierarchical, hash-based structure.
- **Mining**: The process of validating blockchain transactions and adding them to the blockchain by solving cryptographic puzzles (typically in Proof of Work systems).
- **Multi-Signature (Multisig)**: A type of digital signature that requires multiple keys (usually from different parties) to authorize a transaction.


- **Mainnet**: The primary, production blockchain network where real-value transactions occur.
- **MEV (Maximal Extractable Value)**: Value extractable by ordering, including, or censoring transactions during block production.
- **MPC (Multi‑Party Computation)**: Techniques allowing multiple parties to compute/sign jointly without revealing their private inputs.
- **Merkle Proof**: A minimal proof showing that data is included in a Merkle tree using sibling hashes.
## N

- **Node**: A computer or device that participates in the blockchain network by validating transactions and maintaining a copy of the blockchain.
- **Nonce**: A number used once in cryptographic communications, commonly in mining, where miners adjust the nonce to find a valid hash for the next block.
- **NFT (Non-Fungible Token)**: A type of cryptographic token that represents a unique asset. NFTs are not interchangeable and often represent digital art, collectibles, or virtual real estate.


- **Non‑Custodial**: A setup where users retain control of their private keys and assets.
- **Nakamoto Consensus**: Proof‑of‑Work longest‑chain consensus introduced by Bitcoin, tolerating Byzantine failures probablistically.
## O

- **Oracles**: Third-party services that provide external data to smart contracts, allowing them to interact with real-world events (e.g., price feeds, weather data).


- **Optimistic Rollup**: A Layer 2 that assumes transactions are valid and relies on fraud proofs and challenge periods.
- **On‑Chain / Off‑Chain**: Whether data or computation occurs directly on the blockchain or outside it.
- **Opcode**: A low‑level virtual machine instruction (e.g., EVM opcodes) executed by the runtime.
## P

- **Peer-to-Peer (P2P)**: A decentralized interaction where two parties directly transact with each other without intermediaries.
- **Private Key**: A secret key used to access and control cryptocurrency in a wallet. It must be kept secure, as anyone with access to the private key can control the funds.
- **Proof of Work (PoW)**: A consensus mechanism where participants (miners) solve computationally intensive puzzles to validate transactions and secure the blockchain.
- **Proof of Stake (PoS)**: A consensus mechanism where participants (validators) validate transactions based on the number of tokens they hold (staked) in the network.
- **Protocol**: A set of rules that define interactions on a blockchain network, such as transaction processing and consensus mechanisms.
- **Public Key**: A cryptographic key that can be shared publicly and is used to receive cryptocurrency.


- **Permissioned / Permissionless**: Whether participation (validating, transacting) requires approval from an authority or is open to all.
- **Plasma**: A framework for scalable child chains anchored to a parent chain with periodic commitments and exit mechanisms.
- **Price Oracle**: A service or mechanism providing asset prices to smart contracts; a specific type of oracle.
- **Proxy Pattern**: An upgradable-contract design separating storage (proxy) from logic (implementation).
- **Precompile**: A built‑in contract that performs specific operations efficiently (e.g., crypto primitives).
## R

- **Rollup**: A Layer 2 scaling solution that processes transactions off-chain but posts the final result back to the main blockchain, reducing on-chain activity and costs.


- **Reorg (Chain Reorganization)**: Replacement of a portion of the canonical chain by a competing fork when it becomes heavier/longer.
- **Restaking**: Reusing staked assets to help secure additional services or networks, often for extra rewards and risks.
- **RPC (Remote Procedure Call)**: An interface (e.g., JSON‑RPC) for dApps and tools to communicate with blockchain nodes.
- **Rug Pull**: A scam where insiders drain liquidity or abandon a project, causing token value to collapse.
## S

- **Scalability**: The ability of a blockchain network to handle a growing number of transactions efficiently.
- **Sharding**: A method for increasing the scalability of blockchain networks by splitting the network into smaller parts (shards) that process transactions in parallel.
- **Smart Contract**: A self-executing contract with the terms written into code, which automatically enforces and executes when predefined conditions are met.
- **SNARK**: Succinct Non-interactive ARgument of Knowledge (used to create compressed proofs of knowledge)
- **Stablecoin**: A type of cryptocurrency that is pegged to a stable asset like the US dollar to minimize price volatility.
- **Staking**: The act of participating in a Proof of Stake network by locking up cryptocurrency to help secure the network and earn rewards.


- **Schnorr Signature**: A compact signature scheme enabling key and signature aggregation (e.g., used with Bitcoin Taproot).
- **Sidechain**: An independent blockchain bridged to another chain to transfer assets and extend functionality.
- **Signature Aggregation**: Combining multiple signatures into one (e.g., BLS) to save space and verification time.
- **Slashing**: Penalties applied to validators for malicious behavior or downtime in PoS systems.
- **SPV (Simplified Payment Verification)**: Verifying payments using block headers and Merkle proofs without running a full node.
- **State Channel**: An off‑chain mechanism where parties exchange signed messages and settle the final state on‑chain.
- **State Root**: A root hash summarizing the global state (e.g., account/storage) at a given block.
- **Sybil Attack**: An attack where a single adversary creates many identities to gain disproportionate influence.
## T

- **Testnet**: A parallel blockchain used for testing new features, applications, or updates without affecting the main blockchain (mainnet).
- **Token**: A digital asset that represents a certain value or utility within a blockchain network. Tokens can be fungible or non-fungible.
- **TPS (Transactions Per Second)**: A measure of how many transactions a blockchain can process in a given second.


- **TEE (Trusted Execution Environment)**: A hardware‑based secure enclave for isolated computation and key handling.
- **Threshold Signature Scheme (TSS)**: A signature that requires a threshold m‑of‑n of participants to produce a valid signature.
- **Time to Finality**: Elapsed time from transaction submission until economic/irreversible finality is achieved.
- **Turing‑Complete**: Describes a language/VM capable of expressing any computation given sufficient resources.
- **TVL (Total Value Locked)**: The aggregate value of assets deposited in a protocol or ecosystem.
## V

- **Validator**: A participant in a Proof of Stake blockchain who validates transactions and maintains the blockchain by staking tokens.
- **Vault**: A specialized smart contract in DeFi that locks cryptocurrency to earn yield, typically with a strategy for maximizing returns.


- **Vanity Address**: An address that contains a chosen pattern (e.g., a specific prefix).
- **Verifiable Random Function (VRF)**: A function that produces unpredictable randomness with publicly verifiable proofs.
- **Volatility**: The degree of variation in an asset’s price over time.
## W

- **Wallet**: A software or hardware tool used to store, send, and receive cryptocurrencies. Wallets can be custodial (third-party controlled) or non-custodial (user-controlled).
- **Wrapped Token**: A token that represents another asset, typically from a different blockchain, enabling interoperability (e.g., Wrapped Bitcoin, or WBTC, represents Bitcoin on Ethereum).


- **Watchtower**: A service that monitors channels or L2 protocols for disputes and submits evidence on behalf of users.
- **Withdrawal Queue**: A queue that governs the order and timing of withdrawals (e.g., for staked ETH exits).
- **Whitelist / Allowlist**: A list of pre‑approved addresses or contracts allowed to participate in a sale or feature.
## Z

- **Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP)**: A cryptographic method by which one party can prove to another party that they know a value, without conveying any information about the value itself.



- **zkEVM**: A zero‑knowledge rollup architecture compatible with the Ethereum Virtual Machine.
- **zk‑STARK**: A zero‑knowledge proof system that is scalable and transparent (no trusted setup).
## J

- **JIT Liquidity**: ‘Just‑in‑time’ provision of concentrated liquidity to AMMs to capture fees around imminent trades.


## K

- **Key Pair**: A matched public and private key used for encryption and digital signatures.
- **KYC (Know Your Customer)**: Identity verification process used by regulated platforms to comply with AML/CTF requirements.


## Q

- **Quorum**: The minimum number of votes or participants required to make a decision or finalize a block/proposal.


## U

- **Unbonding Period**: The time required to unlock and withdraw staked tokens in PoS systems.
- **UTXO (Unspent Transaction Output)**: The transaction model used by Bitcoin where outputs are spent entirely and create new outputs.
- **Upgradable Contract**: A smart contract architecture that allows logic changes (often via proxies) after deployment.


## X

- **Extended Public/Private Key (xpub/xprv)**: Hierarchical deterministic wallet keys defined in BIP‑32, enabling key derivation trees.


## Y

- **Yield Farming**: Strategically allocating capital across protocols to maximize rewards, fees, and token incentives.
